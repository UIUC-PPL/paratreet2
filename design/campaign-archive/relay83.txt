1.  THE HOP HISTOGRAM -- what sharding would cost in messages
====================================================================

climb_local_hops_log2 counts WITHIN-CHARE hops per climb episode.  Bucket 0 =
the parent was immediately remote (zero local hops); bucket b>=1 = 2^(b-1) to
2^b - 1 hops.  Under a sharded element every one of those hops becomes an
intra-process message.

CPU, 2 reps each, sizes off in both arms:

    arm                      b0        b1       b2      b3    episodes
    A localcomp ON      230,538   131,849    2,077      11     364,475
                        213,676   161,993    2,089       9     377,767
    B localcomp OFF     188,431   142,635   15,146   2,785     348,997
                        203,998   130,736   21,977   2,822     359,533

    total local hops (from the exact counters, not histogram midpoints)
      A   136,122 and 166,265     mean 151,194
      B   190,306 and 191,944     mean 191,125     +26% over A

    share of episodes with ZERO local hops
      A   63.3% and 56.6%         B   54.0% and 56.7%

*** WITH COMPRESSION ON, ABOUT 60% OF CLIMB EPISODES NEVER TAKE A LOCAL HOP AT
    ALL -- the parent is already remote -- and almost all of the rest take
    exactly one.  Episodes of 2-3 hops are 0.57% and 4-7 hops are 10 per run.
    Turning compression off raises total hops only 26%, but it moves the TAIL
    by two orders of magnitude: 2-3 hops x7 to x10, and 4-7 hops from ~10 to
    ~2,800. ***

So B does bound the message volume from above, and the bound is not alarming:
**~151k intra-process messages per run today, ~191k with compression disabled.**

====================================================================
2.  local_union AGAINST TOTAL SUBMISSIONS -- and the GPU arm is a
    COMPLETELY DIFFERENT PROBLEM
====================================================================

Distributed chains = fb1_root, and a flip re-issues union_request so it starts
an extra chain; distinct distributed edges = fb1_root - fb2_flip.
Submissions = those + local_union.

    arm            local_union            distributed          total
    CPU  A         787,000  63.4%         454,271  36.6%     1,241,271
                   794,811  63.6%         455,146  36.4%     1,249,957
    CPU  B         793,667  63.8%         451,208  36.2%     1,244,875
                   791,383  63.7%         450,594  36.3%     1,241,977
    GPU  szON       53,594  11.0%         433,792  89.0%       487,386
                    53,040  10.9%         434,335  89.1%       487,375
    GPU  szOFF      52,917  10.9%         434,463  89.1%       487,380
                    51,718  10.6%         435,676  89.4%       487,394

*** THE GPU ARM SUBMITS 2.6x FEWER EDGES AND HAS ALMOST NO LOCAL FAST PATH:
    487k against 1.24M, and 11% local against 63.5%.  That is the PE-set split
    showing up in the accounting.  FOF_GPU_PHASE1 forces peSets=1
    (src/common.h), so phase 1 already merged every intra-process pair and what
    reaches uf2 is genuinely cross-process.  On CPU, peSets = ppn = 7 defers
    every cross-PE pair inside a process to the walk, and uf2's fast path then
    absorbs 790k of them for free. ***

WHAT THAT MEANS FOR THE SHARDED DESIGN.  Sharding lifts the tail-concurrency
cap from 1 PE per process to ppn, and pays two costs.  They are wildly
different between the arms:

    cost                         CPU arm            GPU arm
    intra-process hop messages   ~151k (<=191k)     ~123k-149k
    local fast path LOST         ~790k edges        ~53k edges
                                 become distributed chains, >=2 messages each

*** ON CPU THE DOMINANT COST IS NOT THE HOPS, IT IS LOSING THE FAST PATH:
    ~790k edges promoted to full two-phase finds against ~151k added hop
    messages, a ratio of about 5 to 1 in edges and worse in messages.  ON GPU
    the fast path is only 53k, so the two costs are comparable and both small.
    Sharding is a far cheaper proposition on the GPU arm than on the CPU arm,
    and the CPU arm's cost is manufactured by the PE-set split rather than
    being intrinsic. ***

A caveat before anyone budgets from these: sharding also CHANGES the numbers it
is being budgeted against.  With more elements per process, pairs that are
same-chare today stop being same-chare, so the local_union count is not a
constant to be subtracted -- it is the thing being converted.  The table bounds
the conversion, it does not predict the post-sharding steady state.

3.  LOCAL PATH COMPRESSION IS WORTH NOTHING ON THE WALL
====================================================================

    A  localcomp ON    4579.5 ms  [4565.7 .. 4593.4]   walk 0.410 s
    B  localcomp OFF   4562.3 ms  [4528.1 .. 4596.4]   walk 0.408 s

Ranges OVERLAP; B is nominally 17 ms faster, which is noise.  Disabling the
compression raises local hops 26% and the 4-7 hop tail 250-fold and costs
NOTHING measurable.

That is the third independent mechanism to shorten chains and buy no time --
after the compression wave (relay77) and the backward short-circuit
(relay78, -32% remote climb hops, wall unchanged).  Chain length is not the
constraint, and this is now measured three different ways.

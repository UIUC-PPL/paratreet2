====================================================================
1.  LEAF INTERACTIONS ARE THE RIGHT UNIT, AND IT MATTERS
====================================================================

Both units were carried in the SAME run, on identical pairs, so they are
directly comparable instead of inferred across two jobs.

    per-PE quantity            r with goDown time    r^2     max/mean
    node-pair visits (relay68)       +0.896          80%       3.74x
    leaf interactions (this)         +0.951          90%       5.15x
    goDown TIME itself                  --            --       6.81x

leafints = sum over leaf-leaf tests of n_particles(src) * n_particles(tgt),
which is the quantity doLeaf already hands to countLeafInts -- the actual
n^2 work. It explains 90% of the variance in walk time against 80% for
visits, and its imbalance (5.15x) is far closer to the truth (6.81x).

*** SO relay68 UNDERSTATED THE PROBLEM.  The real per-PE walk imbalance
    under the current rule is 5.22x, not the 3.79x visits reported. ***

Neither unit is the whole story: time is still more skewed (6.81x) than even
leafints (5.15x). The residue is the cache/fetch component and scheduling,
which no per-pair cost model captures. I am not going to chase it further --
90% is enough to design a weight on, and the remaining gap makes every
number below a mild UNDER-estimate of the available gain.

WHAT CHANGES FROM relay68 AND WHAT DOES NOT:

  CHANGES -- the static rule is worth MORE than I said. Max load falls
  5.22x -> 3.57x, a 32% cut, against the 23% the visits unit implied.

  DOES NOT CHANGE -- every qualitative conclusion:
    - the hash coin gains almost nothing (4.97x), so the parity rule is
      still not the defect;
    - per-PIECE weights gain little (4.51x), per-PE weights are where the
      gain is (3.57x);
    - sub-pair splitting still buys almost nothing over whole pairs
      (2.33x -> 2.12x, 9%), so granularity is STILL not the binding
      constraint -- the two-owner restriction is;
    - no single pair is too big to move: the heaviest is 47.2% of the ideal
      per-PE load and zero pairs exceed it.

  AND ONE THING GOT WORSE: 34 PEs do NO leaf work at all under the current
  rule, and 29 still do none under the greedy bound (against 11 either way
  on visits). The structural floor is more real, not less.

====================================================================
3.  A DESIGN REQUIREMENT, PREDICTED AND THEN HIT
====================================================================

relay67 section 4(ii) warned that a partially-filled weight table would give
w[X]+w[Y] = 0 and divide by zero. That is not hypothetical and it is not
about partial filling: SOME PIECES DO ZERO LEAF WORK. Their pairs are
settled by internal-node pruning alone and never reach a leaf test. The
first run of the simulation died on exactly that, on real data.

    THE RULE MUST USE w = 1 + cost, NOT w = cost.

That is one character in the implementation and a wrong answer if omitted --
with w[X]+w[Y] = 0 the modulo is undefined behaviour in C++, not a trap.

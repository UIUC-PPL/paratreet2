====================================================================
1.  WHAT WAS BEING TESTED
====================================================================
relay93/94/95 showed -s 128 broke the 64->128 scaling stall at 128 nodes.
That left one question: WHERE IS THE OPTIMUM CAP s*, and does it move with P?

Two models were on the record BEFORE the run, which is the only reason this
run can settle anything:

  FAN-IN model (mine).  The cost being capped is the per-process install of
  the starter pack, P x pack_n.  pack_n itself grows with P, so capping
  removes one factor of P.  The right cap is the number of canopy levels the
  walk actually needs, which is a property of the TREE, not of P.
  PREDICTION: s* is P-INDEPENDENT.  cap 128 should win at every scale.

  CHAIN model (the alternative).  The cap should track the per-process share
  of the canopy, which falls as P rises.
  PREDICTION: s* moves as 1/P -- roughly 1024 at 16 nodes, 256 at 64, 128
  at 128.

I said before the run that P-independence was the CONVENIENT outcome and
therefore the one to distrust.  That caution turned out to be worth having.

====================================================================
3.  BOTH MODELS ARE REFUTED
====================================================================
FAN-IN is dead.  It predicted cap 128 best at every scale.  At 16 nodes
UNCAPPED is best and every cap is worse; at 64 nodes cap 128 is the WORST
arm in the sweep.  A model that names the worst arm as the winner is not
salvageable by tuning.

CHAIN is dead too.  It predicted a clean 1024 / 256 / 128 progression.  The
16-node curve is not monotone in the cap at all (32 is better than 128, 512
and 2048), and at 64 nodes the nominal winner 2048 OVERLAPS the uncapped
control [1670.9..1771.7] vs [1753.0..1771.9].  There is no ordered
progression to fit.

I am retracting the fan-in model rather than repairing it.  P does not
cancel, and it does not scale as 1/P.

====================================================================
4.  WHAT THE DATA DOES SUPPORT -- A CROSSOVER, NOT AN OPTIMUM
====================================================================
The shape that survives is not "pick s*" but "cap or do not cap":

  the COST of capping is roughly SCALE-INDEPENDENT.  Capping makes the walk
  materialise the top canopy levels lazily; the walk penalty is 30-140 ms on
  2B and 2-9 ms on 80M, and it does not grow much with P.

  the BENEFIT of capping is bounded by the loadCache it removes, and that
  grows steeply: 11 ms (16n), 102 ms (64n), 357 ms (128n) on 2B, close to
  P^1.6 as relay92 measured.

  cap when the loadCache saving exceeds the walk penalty.

  scale        loadCache   walk penalty   predicted net   measured net
  2B  @ 16n      11 ms        +29 ms         -20 ms        +15  (a LOSS)
  2B  @ 64n     102 ms        +50 ms         +37 ms         -41  (a wash)
  80M @ 16n      13 ms         +2 ms          +9 ms         -11
  80M @ 64n     126 ms         +5 ms        +112 ms        -117

The SIGNS match at all four points and the magnitudes are close at three.
The crossover sits near 64 nodes on 2B, which is exactly where the sweep is
ambiguous -- the model and the ambiguity agree.

CAUTION ON THIS MODEL.  It is a post-hoc fit to four points, from the same
run that killed the two pre-registered models.  It has not predicted
anything yet.  It should be treated as a hypothesis for the 24B/80B runs to
attack, not as a result.

THE KEY NUMBER.  loadCache at 64 nodes is 102 ms on 2B and 126 ms on 80M --
essentially the SAME ABSOLUTE COST for a dataset 25x smaller.  loadCache
depends on P, NOT on N.  As a share of the iteration that is 5.8% on 2B and
33.2% on 80M.

The consequence is visible directly: 80M at 64 nodes UNCAPPED (379.0 ms) is
SLOWER than at 16 nodes (248.1 ms).  Negative scaling.  Capping recovers most
of it (261.6 ms).  A smaller dataset hits the canopy wall at a much lower node
count, because the wall is set by process count while everything else shrinks
with N.

ON CLUSTERING, HONESTLY.  I predicted before the run that clustering, not
size, was where the real dependence would live.  Size is what showed up.  I
cannot separate the two with one alternative dataset -- 80M differs from 2B in
both size and structure -- so I have NO evidence about clustering either way,
and the clustering prediction is neither confirmed nor refuted.  It is
untested.

====================================================================
6.  CONSEQUENCE FOR ITEM 3 (the collect gate)
====================================================================
The composition argument for item 3 assumed s* was P-independent, so that a
fixed depth cut would give a P-independent collected count.  That assumption
is refuted and CANNOT be carried into the depth gate's justification.

Item 3 survives anyway, on a different and better argument: it attacks the
COLLECT, whose cost is O(P) point-to-point messages into a singleton chare
regardless of what the walk needs.  That is a structural defect and does not
depend on where s* is.  See relay97, which measures it.

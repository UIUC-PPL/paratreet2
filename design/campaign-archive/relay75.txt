    events per PE                                  2,668 to 33,309

So it is NOT one long chain funnelling to a boss.  It is 128 PEs, all

    union-find CPU in the drain    1196 PE-ms

That is a distributed latency-bound cascade across 14% of the machine, not a
serial chain.  Every one of those PEs is waiting on remote round trips
almost all of the time, and they finish together.

3.  WHY THE WAVE DID NOT HELP, QUANTITATIVELY
====================================================================

e2a877e fires the wave in runFoFPhase3Dist AFTER t2, at the fireUF2Edges
barrier, and before the QD that drains the remaining cascades.  Its comment
says that QD "is exactly the near-idle tail the wave shortens".

    t_uf2 (= fireUF2Edges + QD + find_components)      ~50 ms in relay65
    walk EP activity ends                              bin 11274
    so the fireUF2Edges barrier sits at roughly         bin 11285
    the drain runs                                     bins 11001..11335

*** ABOUT 284 ms OF THE 334 ms DRAIN HAS ALREADY ELAPSED BEFORE THE WAVE
    FIRES -- roughly 85% of it.  The trace shows a small bump at bins
    11268-11293, which is the barrier plus find_components; everything
    before it is the drain the wave was meant to shorten, already spent. ***

That single fact explains all three relay74 observations without needing any
other mechanism: the wave rewrites only ~256 parents at 2B (the forest is
already largely settled by the time it runs), it costs +50 ms (a broadcast
and cascade over a settled forest), and the drain is untouched (it happened
upstream).

SO THE relay74 VERDICT NEEDS RESTATING.  It is not "the compression wave is
a bad idea".  On this evidence the wave is aimed at exactly the right
structure -- a deep dependence chain that rebalancing cannot fix -- and v1
simply fires at the wrong milestone.  The fireUF2Edges barrier is attractive
because it is census-free, but edge streaming (-E 16) moved the work off it.

BEST CASE IF IT FIRED AT THE RIGHT POINT: the drain is 334 ms of a 4556 ms
CPU iteration, so about 7%.  That is an upper bound, not a forecast -- it
assumes chains collapse to depth <= 2 and the drain collapses with them.

5b.  THE WAVE MAKES THE DRAIN LONGER, NOT SHORTER.

    relay75 cpu-off    (wave off)  drain 348 ms   union-find 1196.0 PE-ms
    relay75 cpu-wave1  (FOF_WAVE=1) drain 409 ms  union-find 1331.0 PE-ms

    +61 ms of drain, and the wall agrees: relay76 measured +57.4 ms at
    -E 16 over two reps.

5c.  I OVER-READ THE BANNER, AND THE CORRECTION MATTERS.

relay74 and my first note last night said the wave "found almost nothing to
compress -- element 0 rewrote 256 parents locally".  THAT READING WAS WRONG.
In unionFindLib.C, `rewrote` counts ONLY element 0, and only the vertices
whose parent happens to be on the SAME element; every remote parent goes out
through wave_need_root_batch and is never counted.  The banner is a tiny
unrepresentative slice, not a measure of the wave's work.

Measured properly from the trace, the wave does a full pass:

    wave_ EPs          1.02 s busy   414,543 calls   122 PEs
    insertDataFindBoss 2.84 s        1,143,803 calls
    union_request      1.51 s           77,495 calls

So it is not idle.  It costs 1022 PE-ms and returns nothing.

5d.  WHY -- AND IT IS NOT ONLY THE POSITION.

The wave's entry methods run in bins 11290..11366, a 77 ms burst.  The drain
runs 10996..11405.  So the wave fires 294 ms into a 409 ms drain -- about
72% of the way through it -- and 100% of the wave's CPU lands inside the
drain window.  The union-find work per 25 ms bucket has already decayed from
363.6 PE-ms at the start of the drain to 27-53 PE-ms by the time the wave
runs.  It arrives at the thin end.

BUT THE POSITION IS NOT THE WHOLE STORY, and this is the result I did not
expect.  relay76 tested the position hypothesis with no code change: -E 0
turns edge streaming off, moving the entire cascade into the uf2 bracket
where the wave fires.

    arm        Iter0      walk     uf2      wave delta
    E16-off   4597.6 ms   0.487 s  0.042 s
    E16-w1    4655.0      0.491    0.090    +57.4 ms  (+1.25%)
    E0-off    4701.7      0.272    0.348
    E0-w1     4730.9      0.267    0.385    +29.2 ms  (+0.62%)
    E0-w2     4809.5      0.271    0.468   +107.7 ms  (hedge)

    for reference, streaming itself is worth -104.1 ms (E0 - E16 = +104.1)

*** WITH THE WHOLE CASCADE MOVED BEHIND THE BARRIER -- uf2 goes 0.042 ->
    0.348 s, an eight-fold deeper bracket sitting directly in front of the
    wave -- THE WAVE STILL COSTS TIME (+29.2 ms) AND STILL REWRITES THE SAME
    ~259 PARENTS ON ELEMENT 0.  Moving the work to the wave did not give the
    wave more to do. ***

THE EXPLANATION THAT FITS BOTH ARMS.  The wave fires at the fireUF2Edges
barrier, which is after edges are SUBMITTED but before the QD that PROCESSES
them.  So:
  - at -E 16 the cascade already ran during the walk and the forest is
    settled and shallow by the time the wave arrives;
  - at -E 0 the cascade has not run YET -- the edges are only submitted --
    so the forest is still trivial and there is nothing to compress.
Either way the wave sees a shallow forest, which is exactly why the count is
the same in both.  There is no single moment at that barrier when the forest
is both DEEP and STABLE.

====================================================================
6.  WHERE THAT LEAVES THE WAVE
====================================================================

1. The target is real and is worth having.  348 ms of a 4700 ms CPU
   iteration (7.4%) and 274 ms of a 2670 ms GPU iteration (10.3%), at under
   1% machine utilisation, in a dependence chain that no rebalancing can
   touch.  Chain shortening is the right idea.
2. v1 as written cannot reach it.  Not merely mistimed: at its barrier the
   forest is shallow under BOTH streaming settings, for two different
   reasons.  Firing it earlier or later at that barrier will not help.
3. To hit the drain the wave would have to fire DURING the cascade -- while
   chains are deep and still forming -- which is precisely where there is no
   barrier, and is why v1 chose the barrier it did.  A census-free trigger
   inside the drain (a timer, a depth probe on the first PE to notice a long
   chain, or a wave fired from the QD's own progress) is the design
   question, and it is a real one rather than a tuning exercise.
4. KEEP THE DEFAULT OFF.  Both modes are correct -- every arm EXACT in
   relay74 and relay76, and the components line bitwise identical -- so the
   knob costs nothing while the trigger is worked out.

ONE CHAIN OR MANY -- ANSWERED, no job needed (section 2).  It is MANY: 128
PEs, all engaged for essentially the whole drain, all still working at the
end, each ~97% idle.  So there is no single critical chain to target and a
targeted fix is off the table -- a GLOBAL mechanism is the right shape after
all, which is what the wave is.  What it needs is a trigger that fires while
those 128 cascades are still deep.

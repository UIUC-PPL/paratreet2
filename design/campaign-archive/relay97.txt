====================================================================
1.  WHAT THE GATE DOES, AND WHY THE WALK CANNOT SEE IT
====================================================================
Kale's catch: -s caps what Driver::loadCache SHIPS but not what it COLLECTS.
Every TreeCanopy element messaged the driver regardless, so all of them
arrived and were sorted before anything was capped.  relay93's trace found
69,670 recvTC invocations ON PE 0 AND ON NO OTHER PE -- 34,835 canopies x 2
accumulation rounds -- inside a 481 ms window.

f9faa0f gates the driver-send on the same -s, keeping WHOLE LEVELS: the
smallest b^(d+1) with (b^(d+1)-1)/(b-1) >= num_share_nodes.  At -s 128 with
branch factor 8 that is want=897 -> limit=4096, i.e. indices < 4096 =
depths 0..3 = 1+8+64+512 = 585 elements, x2 rounds = 1,170 sends.

THE SHIP IS UNCHANGED, AND THAT IS WHAT MAKES THIS A CLEAN A/B.  sortStorage
sorts by key, the element index IS the prefix-coded key, so the shipped
prefix is the 128 smallest indices -- all of which are below 4096 and are
therefore still collected.  Both arms ship the same 128 entries and the walk
sees the same starter cache.  Any Iter0 difference is the COLLECT alone.

That is not just an argument; it is confirmed in the data.  canopy_fills --
the count of canopy entries the walk had to fetch because they were not in
the starter pack -- is 347,600 in the M arm and 347,603 in the G arm.  Three
apart in 347 thousand.  The walk is doing the same work.

====================================================================
3.  I UNDER-PREDICTED THE GAIN, AND THE GAP IS NOT EXPLAINED
====================================================================
Before the run I put ~3% on the record and warned it might not separate.  It
is 8.3% and it separates cleanly.  That prediction was wrong and the reason
is worth stating.

loadCache's own timer moved only 9.7 ms.  Iter0 moved 134.8 ms.  ABOUT 125 MS
OF THE GAIN IS OUTSIDE THE PHASE BEING MEASURED, and it is 3.6x the 37.6 ms
of PE-0 CPU that relay93's trace attributed to recvTC.

The explanation that fits: recvTC arrives during the canopy upward pass,
BEFORE loadCache is entered, so the phase timer never contained it.  PE 0 is
the driver PE; its lateness delays loadCache's start, which delays the ship,
which delays everything after it.  My ~3% estimate was built on the traced
EP time and inherited its blind spot.

WHY 3.6x AND NOT 1x IS NOT SETTLED.  Candidates, none measured here:
  - Charm's traced EP duration excludes receive-side runtime cost (message
    allocation, unpacking, scheduling), so 37.6 ms was always a floor.
  - 69,670 messages converging on ONE process contend for that process's
    poll threads (+lci_ndevices 7) and can delay unrelated traffic in it,
    including walk requests.
  - Queueing delay ahead of the driver rather than CPU time in it.
What would settle it is a traced rep of BOTH arms comparing the window
before loadCache.  Traces are on demand only, so I have not taken one --
say the word if you want it.

I am reporting -8.3% as measured and the MECHANISM as partly unexplained.
The two should not be conflated.

identical ship, identical loadCache, canopy_fills 0 in both.  CONFIRMED.

MU and GU are therefore IDENTICAL CODE PATHS -- and they differ by 68.8 ms.
That is a free measurement of the noise floor at 128 nodes: SINGLE-REP DELTAS
BELOW ~70 MS MEAN NOTHING HERE.  The -134.8 ms result clears it as a 3-rep
paired mean with separated ranges, but the per-rep spread (-236.8 to -22.5)
is wide and the exact magnitude should not be defended.

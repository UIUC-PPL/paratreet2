3.  EVERY RULE, EVALUATED ON THE MEASURED COSTS
====================================================================

303,780 pairs (89.6% of pairs but 99.8% of visits -- the uncovered ones are
the 1-visit tail whose owner PE could not be resolved).  Assumption stated
plainly: a pair costs the same whichever side walks it.  Both sides do the
same pruning; what differs is which half is fetched, so this is an
approximation, not an identity.

    rule                                  max load   max/mean
    MEASURED, what actually ran            147,029     3.79x
    current: parity of index sum           146,946     3.79x   <- reproduced
    hash coin, w = 1                       145,454     3.75x
    weighted coin, w = piece degree        135,717     3.50x
    weighted coin, w = piece visits        137,624     3.55x
    weighted coin, w = PE visits (oracle)  113,022     2.92x
    whole-pair greedy LPT (oracle bound)    72,067     1.86x
    sub-pair splitting (fractional bound)   67,934     1.75x

The simulation reproduces the deployed rule to within 0.06% (146,946 against
a measured 147,029), which is the check that the whole pipeline is sound.

READ THE ROWS IN ORDER, BECAUSE THEY ARGUE WITH EACH OTHER:

  - The hash coin gains NOTHING (3.75x against 3.79x).  So the parity rule is
    not the problem and "the tie-break is algebraic" is not the defect.  It
    is already as good as a fair coin.
  - Weighting by anything per-PIECE gains little (3.50x).  With ~71 pieces
    per PE the piece-level variance averages out before it reaches the PE.
  - Weighting by per-PE load is where the gain is (2.92x), and it needs the
    piece->PE map replicated as well as the weights.  This is the rule I
    would build.
  - But even a perfect oracle assignment of whole pairs stops at 1.86x, and
    allowing pairs to be split fractionally between their two owners only
    reaches 1.75x.

WHY THE FLOOR IS 1.75x AND NOT 1.0x.  A pair can only be executed by one of
its two owners.  That makes this a constrained graph-orientation problem, not
free bin-packing.  11 PEs are touched by no pair at all and no scheme in this
family can give them work; 109 PEs remain above 1.5x of mean even under
perfect fractional splitting.  To go below that floor you would have to
change WHICH PIECES LIVE ON WHICH PE, or allow a third PE to take the pair --
which costs the locality that makes either owner the natural choice.

====================================================================
5.  THE PROXY IS ONLY 80% OF THE STORY, AND IT IS FIXABLE
====================================================================

Per-PE node-pair visits (this job) against per-PE goDown TIME (relay65
traces, same configuration):

    Pearson r = 0.896 over 885 PEs   -> visits explains 80% of the variance
    visits max/mean 3.74x            time max/mean 6.81x

VISITS UNDERSTATES THE IMBALANCE BY NEARLY A FACTOR OF TWO.  The heavy PEs do
not merely do more visits, they do MORE EXPENSIVE ones -- which at leaf 128
is what you would expect, since a leaf-leaf test costs the product of the two
particle counts.  A rule tuned on visits will therefore under-correct.

The fix is free: doLeaf already computes exactly the right quantity --
`stats->countLeafInts(source->n_particles * target->n_particles)`.  Charging
THAT to the pair instead of counting stack pops should track time much more
closely.  I would re-run the instrument with that cost unit before tuning any
weights, because every number in section 3 is an underestimate of the
available gain by an unknown factor between 1 and 1.8.

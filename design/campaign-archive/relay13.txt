+backend_poll_thread: THE FLAG DOES NOT DO WHAT ITS NAME SUGGESTS

12. Read the source before running arm 3, so the arm plan could be fixed
    rather than the result explained afterwards.  Three facts settle every
    poll arm:
      - scheduler.cpp:182 -- progress() is called INLINE at the bottom of
        the scheduler loop by ranks with rank % backend_poll_thread == 0,
        including while idle.  There is NO separate progress thread.
      - convcore.cpp:351 -- values below 1 are clamped UP to 1, so
        `+backend_poll_thread 0` means EVERY rank polls, i.e. MORE
        progress than the recommended 2, not none.
      - comm_backend_lci2.cpp:225 -- device_id = thread_id /
        ceil(nthreads/ndevices), and progress() advances ONLY the caller's
        own device.
    With +ppn 14 +lci_ndevices 7 that is device_id = rank/2:

       setting   pollers            device coverage          measured
         2       ranks 0,2,...,12   ALL 7, one poller each   baseline
         0 -> 1  all 14 ranks       each device twice        +1.3% Iter0,
                                                             uf2_setup
                                                             24 -> 34 ms
        14       rank 0 only        device 0 ONLY            HANGS

13. So arm 3 as specified could not resurrect anything, which is why I
    added arm 4 (+backend_poll_thread 14, one poller per process -- the
    least this flag can express).  It HANGS, twice, at 497 s and 501 s,
    both killed by the 8:00 guard.  But it dies in "* Initializing cache
    managers", before the input is read and before any quiet window
    exists: devices 1-6 are never progressed, so it is a DEVICE-COVERAGE
    DEADLOCK AT STARTUP, not a resurrected stall.  The 8:00 guard did
    exactly its job -- two hangs cost 17 minutes of a 40-minute
    allocation and the other six arms still ran.

14. THE RULE, and it generalises: backend_poll_thread x lci_ndevices = ppn.
    The recommended 2 is not a tuning preference -- with ndevices 7 and
    ppn 14 it is the UNIQUE value that covers every device exactly once.
    This is the same invariant as the old "+lci_ndevices must track +ppn"
    note in BUILDS.md/relay3, seen from the other side.

15. CONSEQUENCE: the progress-starvation direction CANNOT be tested with
    this flag.  Anything below one poller per device breaks startup.  The
    only reachable states are "every device covered once" and "twice".

THE PER-PE DUMP (free on asis-r2) -- IT RETIRES ITEM 26

18. Item 26 proposed a claim priority that charges the MARGINAL CROSS COST
    of taking one more piece, on the argument that cross work is convex in
    pieces-per-PE.  The arithmetic is right and the premise is false:

       pieces vs cross seconds          -0.084
       pieces vs self seconds           -0.246
       pieces vs TOTAL phaseA seconds   -0.237
       self vs cross seconds            +0.318

    Piece count does not predict cross cost and predicts total cost WITH
    THE WRONG SIGN.  PEs holding more pieces finish sooner -- the claim
    pool already works this way, so piece count is an OUTCOME of speed,
    not a cause of load.

19. The PE that sets each process's phaseA wall holds FEWER pieces than
    its peers (33.9 vs 35.8) while taking 16.7% longer.  Piece counts are
    far more skewed than time (within-process max/mean 1.490 for pieces
    against 1.145 for time) and the two skews barely correlate (+0.189).
    Equalising piece counts would make the imbalance WORSE even though it
    would genuinely cut sum p(p-1)/2 by 7.2%.

20. WHAT VARIES IS THE COST OF A PIECE: per-piece phaseA cost runs
    11.7 / 28.6 / 138.9 ms (min/median/max) -- 4.9x from median to worst.
    That is what a claim priority would have to charge, and the count is
    not a proxy for it.

21. Ceiling recomputed from PER-PE data (was process sum/max in item 23):
    sum over processes of max(self+cross) 154.12 s against 133.63 s for a
    perfect within-process split, -13.3%.  The number is unchanged; the
    reachable MECHANISM is not.  VERDICT: do not build item 26 as
    specified.  If the line is worth pursuing, the next instrument is a
    per-PIECE (cost proxy, realized time) dump to see whether the existing
    m2_* load-model terms predict that 4.9x spread.

22. Cross share cross-checks relay12 exactly: 5.47% here from per-PE sums
    against 5.4% there from process sums.

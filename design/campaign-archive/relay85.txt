  Kale: "I want the distribution of roots of components that straddle
  processes, by PEs.  Since union-find makes parent pointers go to smaller
  numbers, maybe they will all concentrate based on the numbering used (but I
  see activity in the tail on PEs like 350, 840, 854)."

  ANSWER: THEY DO NOT CONCENTRATE.  125 of 128 chares root straddling
  components, the distribution is broad with only moderate skew (max/mean
  4.7x, stdev/mean 0.85), and the top half of chares holds 79% rather than the
  50% of a flat distribution.  The numbering does NOT pile the roots onto the
  low chares.

====================================================================
1.  HALF OF THE QUESTION IS SETTLED BY THE ENCODING, NOT BY MEASUREMENT
====================================================================

    FoFPhase1.h:124   uf2EncodeTip(process, dense_index)
                        = (process << kUF2IdxBits) | dense_index

The process index is in the HIGH bits, and find_boss2 refuses to point at a
larger id (the min-heap rule).  So the root of a component is the MINIMUM id
in it, and therefore always sits on the LOWEST-NUMBERED PROCESS that owns any
of its vertices.  That is exact and needs no run.

What it does NOT determine is the shape.  If components straddle only
NEIGHBOURING processes in the SFC ordering then every chare except the last
can be the minimum of some straddling set and the distribution is broad; only
if components straddled far would the mass pile onto low chares.  The
measurement decides between those, and it comes out broad.

One prediction of the encoding IS visible in the data and confirms the method:
**chare 127 roots exactly zero straddling components**, because the highest
chare can never be the minimum of a set that includes a lower one.

====================================================================
2.  THE DISTRIBUTION
====================================================================

Measured after labeling: each element dedupes the component labels it holds
and maps each to its owning chare (the label IS the root's vertexID).  A label
owned by another chare means this chare holds vertices of a component rooted
elsewhere.  Summed over chares, that is how much cross-process structure is
rooted at each chare.

    straddle incidences   264,511 over 128 chares      mean 2,066
      min 0   p10 456   p25 930   median 1,592   p75 2,827   p90 4,472
      max 9,678          max/mean 4.7x      stdev/mean 0.85
      chares rooting any: 125 of 128

    concentration            measured      flat would be
      top  1 chare              3.7%              0.8%
      top  8 chares            21.0%              6.2%
      top 16 chares            34.5%             12.5%

    busiest chares (PE = 7 x chare)
      c48 (PE 336)  9,678     c119 (PE 833) 8,728    c113 (PE 791) 7,501
      c50 (PE 350)  7,124     c47  (PE 329) 6,583    c108 (PE 756) 5,398

*** SO IT IS SKEWED BUT NOT CONCENTRATED.  A flat distribution would give
    every chare 2,066; the real one runs from 0 to 9,678 with a median of
    1,592.  That is the signature of components straddling their SFC
    NEIGHBOURS -- every chare gets to be the minimum of the straddles that
    reach upward from it -- modulated by how much matter sits at each
    process's boundary.  It is not the signature of a numbering artefact. ***

The contiguous near-empty run at c53-c58 is a spatial feature, not a numbering
one: six adjacent processes whose domains have almost nothing straddling
outward.  c127 is the analytic zero from section 1.

====================================================================
3.  KALE'S PEs 350 / 840 / 854 -- ONE OF THE THREE IS A ROOT HOTSPOT
====================================================================

    PE 350 = chare 50    7,124 incidences   4th busiest, 3.4x mean
    PE 840 = chare 120   2,037              essentially the mean (2,066)
    PE 854 = chare 122     560              well BELOW the mean
    (PE 833 = chare 119   8,728             2nd busiest, and it is next door
                                             to 840)

So the tail activity Kale saw is not explained by root ownership alone.  PE 350
is a genuine root hotspot; PE 840 is average and PE 854 is below average, yet
both showed tail activity.  That is consistent with what relay84 found: climb
and label traffic happens wherever the VERTICES are, which is every home PE,
while root ownership is a separate and much more skewed quantity.  Root
ownership is part of the story for PE 350 and is not the story for the other
two.

====================================================================
4.  AN INVARIANCE WORTH HAVING
====================================================================

*** THE PER-CHARE VECTOR IS BITWISE IDENTICAL ACROSS EVERY ARM AND REP --
    CPU and GPU, both reps, 264,511 incidences and the same 128 numbers. ***

That is a real result rather than a formality.  The GPU arm runs with
FOF_GPU_PHASE1, which forces peSets=1, so phase 1 merges every intra-process
pair and far fewer vertices reach uf2 at all.  The LOCAL quantities move
accordingly:

    local roots           CPU 641,908-642,396      GPU 263,677
    distinct labels held  CPU ~906,400             GPU  528,188
    labels rooted elsewhere   264,511                   264,511   <-- identical

The cross-process component structure is therefore INDEPENDENT of the PE-set
split and of the device path: the split changes how much work uf2 is handed
inside a process, and changes nothing about which components cross process
boundaries or where they root.  That also means this distribution is a
property of the decomposition, not of the configuration, and it will not move
if the sharding or nodegroup design changes.

====================================================================
5.  WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE DESIGN
====================================================================

  - There is no root hotspot to relieve.  125 of 128 chares root straddling
    components and the busiest holds 3.7% of the mass.  A design premised on
    breaking up a root concentration has nothing to break up.
  - The drain's ~128 active PEs are active because they are ALL the home PEs,
    one per process -- not because roots cluster.  relay84 already showed those
    PEs are keeping up (53% busy in the walk window, 3.7% in the drain).
  - Combined with relay84, both concurrency arguments for a nodegroup are now
    closed: not tail concurrency, not receiver queuing, and not root
    concentration.  The remaining lever is unchanged -- protocol ROUND-TRIP
    COUNT: fold the two-phase find and kill the 16.5% flips.

NOT MEASURED: how far components straddle (the number of chares each spans).
The incidence count sums (k-1) over straddling components, so a component
spanning 3 chares is indistinguishable here from two spanning 2.  If the span
distribution matters for the design it needs a second census keyed by label.

====================================================================
6.  KALE'S FOLLOW-UP: COMPARE ONLY THE DENSE BITS?
====================================================================

"From a systemic imbalance point of view, will it help if we ignore the
processID (the high order bits) and just consider the dense bits for the '<'
comparison?  Or are smaller dense IDs located within smaller processIDs based
on how we number them?  And does this idea have a chance of breaking the
imbalance (stragglers) of the tail?"

Three answers, and they are all no -- the third one for reasons that are
already measured rather than argued.

(a) THE DENSE BITS ARE NOT GLOBALLY UNIQUE, SO THIS IS A CORRECTNESS BREAK,
    NOT A TUNING CHOICE.  FoFPhase1.h:1136:

        long dense_index = (long)uf2_vertices.size();
        uint64_t encoded = uf2EncodeTip(my_node, dense_index);

    Every process numbers its own vertices 0, 1, 2, ... independently.  Dense
    index 0 exists on all 128 processes.  Comparing only the low 43 bits
    therefore produces massive TIES, and the min-heap needs a strict total
    order.  Look at what find_boss2 would do with a tie between two distinct
    roots u and v with the same dense index:

        dense(u) > dense(v)  is false     -> not a flip
        u != v (full ids differ)          -> UNION: u->parent = v

    and the symmetric message does v->parent = u.  **A two-cycle.**  Every
    subsequent find on that component walks forever.  So a dense-only
    comparator needs a tiebreak, and any tiebreak is just another total order.

(b) NO, SMALL DENSE INDICES ARE NOT IN SMALL PROCESSES.  The dense index is a
    per-process bijection over that process's fragments, taken in
    `frag_counts` map-iteration order -- the comment at :1125 says explicitly
    it need not even be deterministic across runs.  It carries no spatial and
    no cross-process meaning.  There is no correlation to exploit.

(c) IT WOULD NOT BREAK THE TAIL, BECAUSE ROOT SKEW IS NOT WHAT MAKES THE TAIL.
    Suppose the intent is a well-defined randomised order -- hash the full id,
    which fixes (a) and (b) at a stroke and flattens root ownership from
    max/mean 4.7x to about 1.0.  It still does not touch the tail:

      - the home PEs are NOT overloaded in the drain.  relay84: 3.7% busy,
        and 99% of continuous busy runs end within 152 us.  There is no
        straggler to relieve; they are idle, waiting.
      - the total union protocol CPU is 3,388 PE-ms for the whole run
        (relay81, cpu-szOFF).  Even the busiest chare's 3.7% share of the
        straddle mass is ~125 PE-ms spread across a 255 ms drain.  Perfect
        balance would move milliseconds.
      - the drain's wall-to-work ratio is ~280x.  It is round-trip latency in
        a dependence chain, and rebalancing work never shortens a chain.

*** AND IT WOULD PROBABLY MAKE THE THING WE DO CARE ABOUT WORSE.  A flip
    happens when find_boss2 reaches a root whose id is LARGER than the
    incoming boss1 -- the whole two-phase find is discarded and re-issued.
    Measured today: 89,900 flips against ~455k distributed edges, 16.5%.
    A randomised order has no reason to favour either root, so the flip
    probability goes to about 50%.  That is roughly a TRIPLING of flips, each
    one an extra two-phase find, on the exact quantity relay84 identified as
    the remaining lever.  The current numbering is not neutral: union_request
    already orders the two VERTEX ids at submission, and with the process in
    the high bits plus SFC locality the two ROOT ids inherit some of that
    order, which is why 16.5% is so far below 50%. ***

THE CONSTRUCTIVE READING.  The useful direction is the opposite of
randomising: pick an order that makes the FIRST root found more likely to win,
which drives the flip rate DOWN and removes round trips.  Ordering by
something correlated with discovery order -- or folding the two phases so the
question never arises -- both attack round-trip count directly.

CHEAP FALSIFIABLE TEST IF WANTED: a comparator knob (id order vs hashed order)
and one 16-node job.  PREDICTION: root ownership flattens to max/mean ~1.0,
fb2_flip rises from 16.5% toward ~50%, and the wall gets WORSE by roughly the
cost of the extra flips.  If the flip rate does not move, my account of why
16.5% is below 50% is wrong and worth knowing.

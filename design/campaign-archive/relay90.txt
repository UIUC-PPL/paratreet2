*** THE USED COUNT IS CONSTANT.  ~9.6 MILLION NODES AT EVERY DEPTH, varying by
    1.5% across a sweep that changes total fetches by 6.2x.  That is the walk's
    intrinsic working set, and no bundling choice changes it. ***

Everything else follows.  -D 1 fetches 10.2M nodes to use 9.6M -- it wastes
almost nothing (5.5%) and moves only 3.06 GB -- and it is still the SLOWEST of
the first three, because getting there costs 9.57M requests.  -D 3 wastes
76.5% and moves 8.73 GB but needs only 2.64M requests.

That is exactly the trade relay89 priced: requests cost 18.2% of walk wall at
14.9 us each, more PE time than processing the replies (14.0%), so the walk
would rather ship bytes it will never read than ask for them one at a time.
The 76.5% waste is CHEAP and the requests are EXPENSIVE.

    bundle size: 1.06 nodes/request at D=1, 3.9 at D=2, 15.4 at D=3

At the same wall as -D 3, **-D 2 holds 130 MB less per process on average and

Note -D 4's max_MB of 1890 -- 1.9 GB per process for cache alone.  That is the
other reason not to go deeper.

====================================================================
4.  WHAT THIS CLOSES
====================================================================

relay88 concluded the only remaining cache lever was "fetch fewer distinct
nodes".  This sweep tried exactly that, over the knob that controls it, and the
answer is that fetching fewer distinct nodes COSTS more than it saves, because
the working set is fixed at 9.6M and everything below that is paid for in
requests.

*** SO THE CACHE-FETCH REDUCTION LINE IS CLOSED FOR TIME.  Deduplication was
    excluded by construction (relay88), bundling is already at its optimum
    (here), and the working set is invariant.  What remains is making each
    REQUEST cheaper -- which is item 3, the aggregation question. ***

A METHOD NOTE: -D 3 here reads 4553.1 ms against relay82's 4467.2 for the same
configuration in another job.  That is the ~2% between-job spread this campaign
has hit repeatedly; only the within-job comparisons above carry weight.

====================================================================
5.  NOT DONE
====================================================================

Item 3, the aggregation measurement -- splitting requestNodes' 14.9 us into
per-message overhead versus per-key work, by regressing its duration against
the node count it returns, or by separating pending-request joins from full
lookup-and-pack.  It is now the head of the queue, and section 2 makes it more
interesting rather than less: with a fixed 9.6M working set and requests as the
expensive term, per-message overhead is the only cost left that a design change
can reach.

Also not done: any D other than 1-4, the GPU arm, and the m2 dump.

====================================================================

  - D1+aggregation: NOT INDICATED.  Kale's rule was that aggregation is worth
    building only where bytes or memory bind.  At the largest scale we run,

*** THE RESIDUAL IS THE SHIP, NOT THE BARRIER, AND THE EXPONENT PROVES IT.
    The payload grows linearly with P (pack_n P^0.97, because canopies are per
    tree piece) and it is sent to P processes, so bytes out of the origin go as
    P x pack_n ~ P^1.97.  Measured: P^1.89.  A barrier would be P^1.0 or

    finding that the runtime broadcast is a SERIAL RAMP from one origin rather
    than a tree. ***

The residual's SHARE grows with scale too -- 37% at 8 and 16 nodes, 65% at 64,
79% at 128 -- which is why the mechanism was invisible at the scales this
campaign has been working at.  loadCache from 8 to 128 nodes is 89x, worse than
the 49x on record.

    canopy "would address 18.7% of loadCache at best", reasoning that it only
    touches the install.  THAT IS WRONG.  The dominant term is P x pack_n, and
    bounding the canopy caps pack_n -- which removes one whole factor of P and
    turns the ship from O(P^2) into O(P).  It attacks the 78.6%, not the
    18.7%. ***

AND THE KNOB ALREADY EXISTS AND HAS NEVER BEEN SET:

11. TWO CLARIFICATIONS KALE ASKED FOR
====================================================================

(a) HOW EXACTLY DOES CAPPING pack_n GIVE O(P)?

The residual behaves EXACTLY like "the origin ships the whole pack once per
destination process".  Test: if that is the mechanism, then
residual / P / pack_bytes is a BANDWIDTH -- a constant of the machine, not a
fitted parameter.

    nodes     P     pack_B  residual   per-dest   implied B/W
        8    64     310000    0.0015     23.4us      13.2 GB/s
       16   128     590000    0.0041     32.0us      18.4 GB/s
       64   512    2260000    0.0660    128.9us      17.5 GB/s
      128  1024    4460000    0.2807    274.1us      16.3 GB/s

*** 13-18 GB/s ACROSS A 16x RANGE OF P AND A 14x RANGE OF PAYLOAD.  That is a
    constant, and it is a plausible one for a single process pushing through
    +lci_ndevices 7 with 7 of its 1024 destinations on its own node.  The model
    is not fitted; it predicts 285.4 ms at 128 nodes against 280.7 measured. ***

So the cost is

    residual  ~  P  x  pack_n  x  128 B  /  B          two factors of P:
                 ^^^     ^^^^^^                        P destinations, and
                                                       pack_n itself ~ P

Capping pack_n at a constant K removes the SECOND factor and leaves

    residual  ~  P x K x 128 B / B                     = O(P)

*** SO IT IS O(P), NOT O(1) AND NOT O(log P).  The origin still sends one
    message per destination; capping only stops each message from growing.
    Concretely at 128 nodes: 4.46 MB uncapped -> 285 ms, capped at the 8-node
    pack of 2,393 entries (0.31 MB) -> 20 ms.  That is a 14x cut, and it buys
    roughly three or four more doublings before the remaining O(P) becomes the
    blocker again (at 1024 nodes the capped ship is back to ~157 ms). ***

REMOVING THE LAST FACTOR OF P needs a spanning-tree broadcast (O(log P) hops
instead of P sends from one origin) -- a runtime-level change, and exactly what
the recorded "broadcast is a serial ramp, not a tree" finding says we do not
have.  The knob is the cheap fix; the tree is the real one.

(b) IS THE CANOPY COLLECTED ON THE ROOT WITH A CONCAT REDUCTION?

*** NO.  There is no reduction anywhere on the collect side. ***

    TreeCanopy.h:66      d_proxy.recvTC(std::make_pair(this->thisIndex, my_sn));
    paratreet.ci:161     chare Driver { ... }          <- a SINGLETON chare
    Driver.h:460         void recvTC(...) { storage.emplace_back(param); }

Every TreeCanopy element sends its own point-to-point message to one chare,
which appends to a vector.  At 128 nodes that is **69,670 individual entry
invocations on a single PE** -- 34,835 canopies x 2 accumulation rounds (the
initial build and TreePiece::upwardPass, which is why raw_canopies is exactly
twice deduped).

*** SO THE DRIVER IS SERIALISED TWICE, AND I HAVE ONLY MEASURED ONE OF THEM.
      COLLECT   69,670 point-to-point messages INTO one chare  -- UNMEASURED,
                and it happens during the tree build and upwardPass, so it is
                hidden inside phase1, not inside loadCache
      SHIP       P sends OUT of one chare                      -- measured,
                78.6% of loadCache ***

PREDICTION, worth one trace and not yet run: at ~2 us per entry invocation the
collect side is ~0.14 s of driver-PE time at 128 nodes -- the same order as
loadCache itself, and invisible in the phase rows because it is spread through
phase1.  It should show as a hot single PE (the Driver's) in a 128-node trace.

AND THE FIX IS ALREADY HALF-BUILT.  TreeCanopy ALREADY aggregates up its own
tree -- the line right after the recvTC is
`thisProxy[thisIndex / branch_factor].recvData(my_sn, branch_factor)`.  So the
canopy tree is being walked upward in parallel AND every node is separately
messaging the driver.  A concat reduction over the TreeCanopy array, or simply
taking the result at the canopy root that already exists, would replace 69,670
serialised messages with one aggregation -- and would also produce the pack
already deduped, removing the sort and the 2x duplication in one move.

12. THE RIGHT FRAMING IS AN ALL-GATHER (Kale, 2026-08-23)
====================================================================

*** COLLECT AND SHIP ARE NOT TWO PROBLEMS.  THEY ARE ONE ALL-GATHER, AND I HAD
    THE WRONG FRAME.  Section 11 proposed two separate fixes -- a concat
    reduction inward, a spanning-tree broadcast outward.  Kale's reading is
    better: every process CONTRIBUTES its canopies and every process NEEDS all
    of them.  That is an all-gather, and the current implementation routes it
    through a SINGLETON CHARE -- a gather-to-one followed by a
    broadcast-from-one, which is the textbook worst way to do it. ***

IT ALSO EXPLAINS THE EXPONENT WITHOUT ANY EXTRA ASSUMPTION.  An all-gather done
as gather-to-one-then-broadcast-from-one costs P x (total payload) at the
origin; the payload is itself proportional to P; hence P^2, and section 9
measured P^1.89.  A proper all-gather is O(total payload) per participant with
no single serialisation point.

WHY NODE-AWARENESS IS WORTH MORE HERE THAN USUAL.  At 128 nodes there are 1024
processes on 128 physical nodes, 8 per node.  A two-level all-gather -- shared
memory within a node, then 128-way across -- cuts wire traffic and per-endpoint
message count by 8x.  And the payload is SMALL in absolute terms: 4.46 MB
total, 34,835 entries of 128 B.  **This is not a bandwidth problem, it is a
topology problem**, which is precisely the case where a node-aware collective
pays and a bigger pipe does not.

THREE THINGS A CHARM++ IMPLEMENTATION WOULD WANT TO KNOW, all measured:

  1. THE REDUCTION STRUCTURE ALREADY EXISTS.  TreeCanopy aggregates upward on
     its own tree -- `recvData` runs on 895 of 896 PEs in parallel
     (TreeCanopy.h, the line right after the recvTC).  Only the COPY to the
     Driver funnels.  So the inward half is redundant work, not missing work.

  2. THE PAYLOAD ARRIVES IN TWO ROUNDS.  Canopies are sent during the tree
     build AND again after upwardPass, the second superseding the first --
     which is why raw_canopies is exactly 2x deduped (69,670 -> 34,835 at 128
     nodes, 9,258 -> 4,629 at 16).  An all-gather interface must either run
     twice or defer until the annotations are final.  Today `sortStorage`
     absorbs it with a stable_sort plus keep-newest dedup, which is also why a
     proper all-gather would REMOVE the sort: the pack would arrive deduped.

  3. THE ENTRY IS 128 BYTES (std::pair<Key, SpatialNode<Data>>), so the
     all-gather is a fixed-size-element gather -- the easy case.

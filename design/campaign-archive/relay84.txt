  THE VERDICT, up front: THE RECEIVING PEs ARE KEEPING UP, COMFORTABLY.  The
  raw creation-to-begin gaps look enormous (median 16.7 ms in the walk window)
  but they are dominated by cross-process CLOCK OFFSET, which the bidirectional
  test below measures at p90 10.6 ms and up to 66 ms -- the same order as the

  So by Kale's own decision rule this is the second branch: **chain concurrency
  joins chain length as a closed line, and the next lever is protocol
  round-trip count -- fold the two-phase find, eliminate the flips.**

====================================================================
1.  THE TRAP, AND I DID FALL INTO THE SECOND ONE
====================================================================

Kale warned about the projlog field: on BEGIN_PROCESSING, field 6 is the SOURCE
PE and the executing PE is the file the record came from.  Verified before
using it and handled correctly -- match key is (source_pe, event), and event
ids are unique per PE (19,873 creations, 19,873 distinct ids on the PE I
checked).  One match was verified by hand end to end: creation 10,719,207 on
PE 14 event 6635 -> begin 10,720,812 on PE 7, gap 1,605 us.

*** THE ONE I DID FALL INTO WAS CLOCKS, and Kale called it mid-analysis.  My
    first pass reported find-gap medians of 12.9 ms (walk) and 24.3 ms (drain)
    and I was about to read them as queuing.  Two things say otherwise: the
    label group produced NEGATIVE gaps -- messages arriving before they were
    sent -- and the receiver-side numbers in section 3 cannot support waits of
    that size. ***

Also worth recording: there are NO same-PE find messages to use as a
clock-free control.  A same-chare find never becomes a message -- find_boss1
climbs locally in a while loop and only calls boss_send when it LEAVES the
chare -- so src != dst always.  That control does not exist; section 3 is the
substitute.

====================================================================
2.  HOW BIG THE SKEW IS -- the bidirectional test
====================================================================

For a pair with true one-way floor L and clock offset d,
    min(A->B) = L + d      min(B->A) = L - d
so (minAB + minBA)/2 estimates L and |minAB - minBA|/2 estimates d.
593 bidirectional home-PE pairs in cpu-szON:

    minAB + minBA  (= 2L, and it is >= 0 for all 593 -- the minima are
                    self-consistent, so the matching is sound)
      p01 8    p50 208    p99 86,492    max 131,951  us
    |minAB - minBA|/2  (= the clock offset)
      p50 90   p90 10,596  p99 38,498    max 65,977  us

*** MEDIAN TRUE ONE-WAY FLOOR: about 104 us.  MEDIAN CLOCK OFFSET: about
    90 us -- but the offset tail is 10.6 ms at p90 and 66 ms at the worst pair.
    A quantity with a 66 ms error bar cannot be used to measure a delay that
    matters at the tens-of-microseconds scale.  Per-pair minimum subtraction
    removes a CONSTANT offset, and it barely moved the distribution
    (16,682 -> 16,531 us at the median), which says the residue is drift or
    real variation and the two cannot be separated with these timestamps. ***

====================================================================
3.  THE CLOCK-FREE ANSWER: ARE RECEIVERS KEEPING UP?  YES.
====================================================================

Everything here is one PE's own clock, so no cross-process comparison is
involved.  128 home PEs (verified: only multiples of ppn=7 execute
insertDataFindBoss at all -- PEs 1, 2, 8, 13, 890, 895 execute exactly zero).

    cpu-szON, WALK window (bins 10697..10989, 293 ms)
      home-PE busy fraction                     53.4%
        of which  walk EPs 15.6%   cache 26.5%   union-find 9.7%
      execution durations      p50 2 us   p90 14 us   p99 74 us   max 13.2 ms
      continuous busy runs     p50 7 us   p90 44 us   p99 152 us  max 44.4 ms
      per-PE longest busy run  p50 7.2 ms   p90 27.4 ms   max 44.4 ms

    cpu-szON, DRAIN window (bins 10990..11300, 311 ms)
      home-PE busy fraction                      3.7%
      continuous busy runs     p50 44 us  p90 377 us  p99 3,385 us

    cpu-szOFF, WALK window          busy 52.7%, runs p50 7 / p90 46 / p99 163 us

*** A MESSAGE CANNOT WAIT IN A QUEUE LONGER THAN ITS RECEIVER IS CONTINUOUSLY
    BUSY -- an idle PE has an empty queue.  99% of busy runs end within 152 us
    and the median run is 7 us.  The receiver is going idle constantly.  It is
    keeping up, and it is not close to saturated: 53% busy in the walk window
    and 3.7% in the drain, serving items whose median cost is 2 us. ***

Note the run figures are sensitive to how a "break" is defined.  Treating gaps
under 50 us as continuous gives p99 19.6 ms and per-PE longest p50 30 ms;
treating any measurable idle as a break gives p99 152 us.  The strict figure is
the right one for this question, because the scheduler will pick up a queued
message in any real idle moment.  Both are reported so the sensitivity is
visible rather than hidden.

====================================================================
4.  WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE NODEGROUP DESIGN
====================================================================

Kale's framing: the drain PEs are 97% idle, so tail concurrency cannot be the
payoff; the hypothesis was queuing behind walk executions during the
walk-concurrent cascade.

  - TAIL CONCURRENCY: already excluded by Kale, and confirmed here -- home PEs
    are 3.7% busy in the drain.
  - QUEUING BEHIND WALK EXECUTIONS: NOT SUPPORTED.  The home PE is 53% busy in
    the walk window, its executions have a 2 us median, and its queue provably
    empties within 152 us in 99% of busy runs.  There is no backlog to relieve.
  - REAL DELIVERY LATENCY: the floor is about 104 us one-way for the median
    pair.  That is real, but a nodegroup does not remove it -- vertex ownership
    stays with the PROCESS, so cross-process messages remain cross-process.
    Sharding only converts within-chare local hops into intra-process messages,
    which relay83 counted at ~151k per run and which are ADDED cost, not saved.

*** SO THE NODEGROUP IS NOT JUSTIFIED BY ANYTHING MEASURED HERE.  Chain
    concurrency joins chain length as a closed line.  Three mechanisms have now
    shortened chains and bought nothing (the wave, the backward short-circuit,
    disabling local path compression), and now the concurrency argument fails
    on the receiver side as well. ***

THE NEXT LEVER IS ROUND-TRIP COUNT, exactly as Kale set out.  The census gives
the target directly (relay78/83): every distributed edge costs a two-phase
find -- find_boss1 climbs to v's root, then find_boss2 climbs to w's root --
and 16.5% of chains are FLIPS that throw the whole two-phase find away and
re-issue it because the two ROOT ids arrived in the wrong order.  At ~455k
distributed edges per run that is ~90k wasted round-trip pairs.  Folding the
two phases and ordering by root id are both round-trip reductions, and
round-trips at a 104 us floor are what the drain is actually made of.

====================================================================
5.  WHAT I DID NOT ESTABLISH
====================================================================

  - The true MEDIAN delivery latency.  Only the floor (about 104 us) is
    measurable; the median is buried under clock offsets of the same
    magnitude.  Getting it would need synchronised timestamps, which these
    traces do not have.
  - The GPU arms were run through the raw script before the clock problem was
    understood; their numbers are in the same units and carry the same defect,
    so they are not quoted here.  The receiver-side argument in section 3 is
    what matters and it does not depend on them.
  - Whether the ~104 us floor is transport or scheduler pickup.  It is a floor
    either way, and neither is addressed by a nodegroup.

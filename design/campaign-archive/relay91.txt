1.  THE COST MODEL, AND IT VALIDATES ITSELF ACROSS THE SWEEP
====================================================================

Pairing needs no cross-PE matching: a requestNodes execution CREATES its
addCache reply inside its own BEGIN..END block, so one sequential pass over a
server log pairs them.  Nodes inferred at 214 B/node (relay88).

    duration_us = a + b * nodes_in_reply,  fitted independently per depth

    -D   mean nodes/reply   a (fixed, us)   b (per-node, us)   R^2    mean dur
     1        1.48             2.585            1.098         0.075     4.21
     2        4.32             3.074            0.891         0.452     6.92
     3       14.13             2.655            0.847         0.558    14.62
     4       18.44             2.872            0.839         0.894    18.34

*** FOUR INDEPENDENT FITS, ACROSS A 12x RANGE OF REPLY SIZE, GIVE THE SAME TWO
    CONSTANTS: a ~ 2.6-3.1 us and b ~ 0.84-1.10 us/node.  The D=3 fit predicts
    D=4's mean at 2.655 + 0.847*18.44 = 18.27 us against 18.34 measured, and
    D=1's at 3.91 against 4.21.  The linear model is right, and the 14.9 us of
    relay89 decomposes cleanly. ***

(R^2 is low at D=1 only because the reply size barely varies there -- there is
almost no x to regress against.  The intercept is still consistent.)

ALSO MEASURED, AND IT REMOVES THE OTHER HALF OF KALE'S PROPOSED METHOD:
**100% of requestNodes executions created a reply, at every depth.**  There are
no pending-request joins to contrast against -- every request does a full
lookup-and-pack.  So request coalescing is not happening today at all, which is
itself the answer to "is there already de-facto aggregation": no.

====================================================================
2.  WHAT AGGREGATION COULD BUY -- and why that is not enough
====================================================================

At -D 3, the shipping configuration:

    requestNodes per PE           43.7 ms     = 18.2% of walk wall (relay89)
    of which the fixed term       18.2%       = 7.95 ms per PE
    a 4-item buffer removes <=3/4 of that     = 5.96 ms per PE
    as a share of the walk window (~240 ms)   ~ 2.5%
    as a share of a 4525 ms iteration         ~ 0.13%, and only if PE time
                                                converted 1:1 to wall, which
                                                it does not -- PEs are 53% busy

*** SO THE CEILING IS SINGLE-DIGIT MILLISECONDS ON THE ITERATION.  Not worth a
    build.  The spread numbers made aggregation VIABLE (median channel fills a
    4-item buffer in 1.72 ms); this measurement makes it POINTLESS at the
    depth we ship. ***

*** ONE COMBINATION THE TWO MEASUREMENTS TOGETHER DO SUGGEST, and I flag it as
    arithmetic rather than a result.  The fixed term's SHARE is not constant --
    it is 61.5% at -D 1 and 15.7% at -D 4, because the pack grows with depth
    and the overhead does not.  Total request-serving time per PE:

        -D 1   10,683 req x  4.21 us = 45.0 ms
        -D 2    5,477 req x  6.92 us = 37.9 ms
        -D 3    2,951 req x 14.62 us = 43.1 ms
        -D 4    4,091 req x 18.34 us = 75.0 ms

    At -D 1, aggregation would remove 3/4 of 61.5% of 45.0 ms = 20.8 ms per PE,
    taking -D 1's request cost to ~24 ms, well BELOW every other depth -- and
    -D 1 fetches 10.2M nodes instead of 40.7M, wastes 5.5% instead of 76.5%,
    and holds 628 MB/process instead of 838.  Aggregation is worthless at the
    depth we ship and would be worth the most at the depth we rejected.
    THE RISK IS THE SAME BUFFER: -D 1 already has the longest walk (0.580 s
    against 0.448) because of round trips, and a 4-item buffer adds ~0.65 ms
    of flush latency per channel at that depth.  Whether that kills it is
    unmeasured, and it is the only reason I would not simply recommend the
    pair. ***

====================================================================
3.  THE -D 4 ANOMALY -- deeper sharing ships INTERIOR STRUCTURE
====================================================================

Kale's hypothesis was that at depth 4 subtrees bottom out at leaves, so the
extra depth ships particles rather than nodes.  The decomposition says the
opposite about which content grows:

                          -D 3           -D 4        ratio
    interior nodes     11,438,781     35,073,778     x3.07
    cached leaves      29,295,354     28,355,621     x0.97   (FLAT, slightly down)
    cached particles  263,143,034    319,046,426     x1.21
    nodes per request        15.4           17.3
    requests             2,644,471      3,665,431     x1.39

*** THE EXTRA DEPTH SHIPS INTERIOR NODES, NOT LEAVES AND NOT MAINLY PARTICLES.
    Interior structure TRIPLES while the leaf population is flat to within 3%.
    So the shipped subtrees are NOT bottoming out -- they are getting deeper in
    branch structure, and the branch structure is what nobody examines: `never`
    rises 76.5% -> 84.7% while `used` stays at ~9.6M. ***

THE SECOND HALF OF THE HYPOTHESIS IS SUPPORTED, and it explains the
non-monotone request count.  A depth-D slice of an oct tree has a frontier that
grows with the branch factor per level, and the walk's useful coverage does
not grow with it -- the working set is fixed at 9.6M nodes (relay90 section 2).
So going 3 -> 4 multiplies the frontier faster than the coverage it buys, and
each frontier node is a placeholder that the walk may still have to request.
Requests rise 39% even though each reply is 12% bigger.

    particles per cached leaf: 8.98 at -D 3, 11.25 at -D 4.  The leaf MIX
    shifts toward fuller leaves, which is why particles rise 21% on a flat
    leaf count.

*** AND THE FIELD THAT WOULD SETTLE IT IS THE ONE THE LABELLING BUG HIDES.
    The placeholder population is exactly `used_nodes` -- "pool slots incl.
    placeholders", TreeCache.h:237 -- and that is the field FoF3.C:783 packs
    over with cached_nodes and never reports (relay88 section 2).  Fixed
    upstream at 6b2b623 for the label; reporting the real used_nodes as well
    would make this measurable directly instead of inferred. ***

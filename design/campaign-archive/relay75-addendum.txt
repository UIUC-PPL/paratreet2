2026-08-22 08:48.  Corrects section 5 of relay75.txt, which you already have.
Nothing re-measured; every number in relay75 stands.  This is a correction to

--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT relay75 SECTION 5 SAID
--------------------------------------------------------------------

"There is no single moment at that barrier when the forest is both DEEP and
STABLE."

Kale asked what "stable" was doing there.  It was doing something I cannot
defend.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IT IS WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------

I meant: the forest must not be substantially rewritten by unions arriving
after the pass, or the shortening the wave does is not the shortening later
finds traverse.  The word came from the -E 16 side, where I had written the
forest is "settled and shallow", and I generalised "settled" to "stable".
They are not the same idea.

Path compression is not invalidated by later unions.  It degrades one level
at a time.  If the wave points vertex x directly at root R, and R is later
unioned under R', a find from x costs TWO hops, not the original chain
length.  Compression done on a forest that is still changing keeps most of
its value.  Strict stability is not required.

And relay75 section 6 point 3 already contradicts it: it asks for a trigger
that fires DURING the cascade, "while chains are deep and still forming".  A
forest that is still forming is not stable.  Section 5 and section 6 were in
tension and section 5 is the one that is wrong.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE REQUIREMENT ACTUALLY IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Two conditions, neither of them stability:

  1. DEEP           -- chains are long at that moment, or there is nothing
                       to shorten.
  2. PAYBACK AHEAD  -- enough find traversals are still to come for the pass
                       to repay its own cost.

The two failure modes at the fireUF2Edges barrier, restated:

  -E 16   the cascade already ran during the walk.  Chains are short AND the
          traversals that would have benefited are already done.
          BOTH conditions fail.

  -E 0    the edges are only submitted, not processed.  Condition 2 HOLDS --
          all the work is still ahead -- but condition 1 fails, the forest is
          trivial and there is nothing to compress yet.

This is a better account of the relay76 result than the one in section 5: it
explains why the parent count came out the same (~259) in both arms even
though the uf2 bracket in front of the wave was eight times deeper at -E 0.
Depth, not stability, is what was missing there.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
THE REAL CONCERN THAT THE WORD WAS GESTURING AT
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Something legitimate is buried in it, but it is about COST, not structure.
The wave is a full global pass: 414,543 calls, 1.02 s of CPU across 122 PEs,
measured from the relay65 traces.  That cost is paid whether or not the
forest is deep.  So firing early into a still-forming forest is not wrong in
the sense of being undone -- it is wrong in the sense that you may pay a full
pass for little depth, and then need to fire again.

The constraint is therefore cost per firing and how many firings are needed.
That argues for a cheaper, partial, or repeatable mechanism rather than for
one perfectly timed global pass -- and a repeatable cheap pass is a much
easier thing to trigger than a single correct moment.  The design question in
relay75 section 6 point 3 stands unchanged; this only removes a constraint I
had wrongly placed on it.

  - The verdict: FOF_WAVE v1 cannot reach it; keep the default OFF.

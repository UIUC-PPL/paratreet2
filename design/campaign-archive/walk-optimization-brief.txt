Written 2026-08-21, revised 20:30 with the measured rule form.  Self-contained: you do not need
relay66-69 to use this, but they are the evidence and are in uploads/.

All numbers: FoF3, 2B particles, 16 Frontier nodes, 896 PEs, GPU production
shape (ppn 7, PEMAP7, -l 128, affinity fix on), unless the CPU arm is named.

2.  WHAT THE WALK IS MADE OF
====================================================================

Measured directly (job 5324186), not modelled:

    distinct tree-piece pairs walked        338,927
    node-pair examinations                   34.76 million
    leaf interactions (sum n_src * n_tgt)    48.17 billion   <- the real work
    per-pair leaf interactions: half of all pairs do ZERO leaf work
                                heaviest single pair 25.4 million

Machine utilisation DURING the 581 ms walk window:
    goDown itself       47,968 PE-ms   (4.3% of the window's PE-ms)
    cache service       71,011 PE-ms   (13.8%) -- requestNodes + addCache
    everything          119,515 PE-ms  (23.2%)
    last 349 ms of the window: 1.8% busy, 31 PEs active of 896

*** THE WALK NEVER USES MORE THAN 45% OF THE MACHINE AT ANY INSTANT, and
    spends 71% of its window below 25% participation.  It is not a phase
    that starts wide and narrows; it is thin throughout. ***

*** AND THE CACHE SERVICE IS 1.5x THE WALK ITSELF.  Any idea that changes
    how many remote nodes are fetched is working on a bigger number than
    the walk's own compute. ***

====================================================================
3.  WHAT THE TIE-BREAK IDEA DOES, AND ITS HARD CEILING
====================================================================

The dual walk is symmetric: pair (X,Y) can be executed by X's PE or Y's.
Today the choice is parity of the index sum -- balanced in COUNT, blind to
LOAD.  Result: per-PE walk load 5.22x max/mean, 34 PEs doing no leaf work.

Every candidate evaluated offline against the measured per-pair costs:

    current parity rule                     5.22x
    hash coin (randomise, same idea)        4.97x   <- randomising alone is
                                                      worth almost nothing
    weighted by piece degree                4.51x
    weighted by piece cost                  4.62x
    weighted by PE cost                     3.57x   <- best implementable
    whole-pair greedy assignment (bound)    2.33x
    sub-pair splitting (bound)              2.12x

THE RULE, in the only form that is correct (see relay67 section 2):

    lo = min(pieceX, pieceY);  hi = max(pieceX, pieceY)   // TREE PIECES
    a  = pe[lo];               b  = pe[hi]                // their PEs
    winner = ( mix64(lo,hi) % (w[a]+w[b]) ) < w[b] ? lo : hi
    keep   = ( winner == myPiece )

Compute the WINNER, not "do I keep it" -- the latter has both sides
comparing the same hash against different thresholds, which yields
neither-keeps (lost edges) on 31% of pairs.  Hash over PIECES, weight over
PEs: hashing over PEs instead measures 6.13x, WORSE than the current rule.
No per-pair communication; w is a replicated 896-entry integer table and
pe[] a 63,946-entry map, built once by reduction + broadcast.
w = 1 + cost, never w = cost: some pieces do zero leaf work.

Weight source matters and none of the obtainable ones reach the oracle:

    leaf interactions, same run (oracle)    3.57x   unobtainable
    measured feedback, prior run           ~3.8x    needs a prior run
    pair degree (one canopy pre-pass)       4.30x   self-contained
    none, i.e. today                        5.22x

Translated into the iteration:

    weighted coin, w = 1 + PE cost     -184 ms    -6.4%
    with pair-degree weights           -120 ms    -4.2%
    absolute ceiling of the whole idea -345 ms   -12.1%

*** THIS IDEA MOVES WORK, IT DOES NOT REMOVE IT.  Total leaf interactions
    (48.17e9) and total cache fetches are INVARIANT under any tie-break.
    That is the key fact for composing. ***

====================================================================
4.  HOW TO COMPOSE -- AND WHERE YOU WOULD DOUBLE-COUNT
====================================================================

COMPOSES ADDITIVELY (different quantity, different phase):
  - anything that REDUCES total work: better pruning, a cheaper leaf test,
    a larger effective leaf, fewer surviving pairs.  The tie-break then
    redistributes a smaller total, and the percentages multiply rather than
    add.
  - anything in phaseA (CPU arm) or Pre-traversal (GPU arm).  Disjoint.
  - anything that reduces CACHE FETCHES.  71,011 PE-ms against the walk's
    47,968 -- this is the larger target and it is untouched by assignment.

COMPETES FOR THE SAME MILLISECONDS (do not add the percentages):
  - any other scheme that redistributes walk work: work stealing, dynamic
    handoff, a different piece->PE map, load-balancing the TreePieces.
    All of them are drawing on the same 581 ms window, and the combined
    ceiling is the -12.1% above, not the sum of the parts.
  - anything targeting the walk TAIL specifically.  The tail is 71% of the
    window but only 24% of the walk's work; two ideas aimed at it are aimed
    at the same 345 ms.

CHANGES THE GROUND UNDER THIS ANALYSIS (re-measure before trusting section 3):
  - changing -l.  Leaf interactions scale with leaf size, so the pair cost
    distribution and therefore the best weights change.  GPU optimum is 128
    (12 costs +27.7%, 384 costs +52.8%); CPU optimum is 32.
  - changing the decomposition or the number of TreePieces.  The 5.22x is a
    property of which pieces landed on which PE.
  - moving phase 1 off the GPU, or moving the walk onto it.

THE STRUCTURAL FLOOR, which no assignment scheme can pass:
  a pair can only be executed by one of its TWO owners.  34 PEs are touched
  by no leaf-producing pair at all and 29 still are not under a perfect
  greedy assignment.  To use those PEs the piece->PE MAPPING has to change.
  If the other idea touches decomposition or LB, it can reach headroom the
  tie-break cannot -- that is the one place the two ideas genuinely add.

6.  RELIABILITY OF THE ABOVE
====================================================================

  - Per-pair costs are MEASURED, not modelled, and the rule simulation
    reproduces the deployed parity rule exactly, which is the check that the
    pipeline is sound.
  - Leaf interactions explain 90% of the variance in per-PE walk TIME
    (r 0.951).  The residue is cache/fetch and scheduling.  Per-PE leafints
    imbalance is 5.15x where TIME imbalance is 6.81x, so section 3 is a mild
    UNDER-estimate of what perfect balance would win.
  - Every arm of every job cited here produced components 424897832 and
    max_size 185317566 EXACT.
  - The 581 ms walk window sits inside a 1215 ms traversal phase, so walk
    savings reach the iteration only insofar as the walk is on the critical
    path.  Utilisation collapsing to 1.8% during the tail is good evidence
    that it is; it has not been proven by a direct experiment.

Evidence: relay66 (walk tail), relay67 (rule design), relay68 (per-pair
instrument, visits), relay69 (leaf interactions, supersedes relay68 s3).

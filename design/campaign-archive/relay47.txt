1.  SO WHAT IS THE WALK'S PROBLEM AT ppn 14?  Not the split.  The phases split
    cleanly by character:

      phase          ppn14/sets7   ppn7/sets7   delta
      phaseA            4.778        4.619      -159 ms   -3.3%
      phase3_walk       2.483        1.429     -1054 ms  -42.4%

    phaseA is essentially SMT-NEUTRAL: dense local per-PE work, and twice the
    hardware threads buys it 3%.  The phase-3 walk is SMT-HOSTILE: it loses
    42% when the two hardware threads of a core share it.

    HYPOTHESIS, LABELLED AS ONE.  The walk is a pointer-chasing tree traversal
    and therefore memory-latency-bound and cache-resident-set sensitive; two
    SMT siblings on one physical core halve the effective L1/L2 each thread
    sees and serialise on the same load pipeline.  phaseA is compute-dense and
    does not care.  That is consistent with everything here and with the GPU
    arm, where the walk was also where ppn 7's win lived -- but nobody has
    measured cache or memory counters, and I am not claiming it as established.

2.  ONE MORE THING THE MIDDLE ARM SHOWS.  Forcing sets 7 at ppn 14 brings
    phaseB back: 0.000 s at sets 14, 0.113 s at sets 7.  Removing phaseB is
    exactly what the PE-set split is for, so the split IS doing its job at
    ppn 14 -- it is just that the 113 ms it saves is irrelevant against the
    1575 ms the thread count costs.  The AUTO default is not the problem and
    should not be changed on the strength of relay46.

3.  THE ANSWER TO THE ORIGINAL QUESTION.
    CPU-only, current defaults, 2B on 16 nodes:  ppn 7 beats ppn 14 by
    1575-1644 ms, 16.5-17.2%, in two independent jobs, with non-overlapping
    ranges in both.  It is a thread-count effect, it lives in the phase-3
    walk, and it is NOT an artefact of the AUTO PE-set default.

    So the GPU and CPU recommendations converge on the same launch shape, but
    FOR DIFFERENT REASONS, and both should be stated that way:
      GPU arm: ppn 7 wins because the SMT siblings are free for ROCm's helper
               threads to land on -- headroom the fix exploits.
      CPU arm: ppn 7 wins because the phase-3 walk does not tolerate SMT.
    The ppn-14 idiom in the machines docs and the README is not right as a
    default on this machine at this scale, for either arm.

4.  WHAT WOULD MAKE THE HYPOTHESIS IN SECTION 1 A RESULT.  Run the walk alone
    under `perf stat` at both shapes and compare L2/L3 miss rates and
    cycles-per-instruction, or run ppn 14 pinned to 14 PHYSICAL cores across
    two processes rather than 7 cores' SMT siblings -- if the penalty vanishes
    it is SMT, if it survives it is PE count.  The second is a pemap change
    and one job.

relay66 -- REASSIGNING SYMMETRIC SUBTREE PAIRS AT THE WALK TAIL.
Kale's question, 2026-08-21.  Feasibility, utility, clarification.
Measured on the relay65 traces (job 5322240), both arms, 896 PEs.

====================================================================
0.  THE SHORT ANSWERS
====================================================================

FEASIBILITY: yes, and cheaper than it sounds.  The unit of work is ALREADY
a (source node, target node) pair on a stack; the tie-break is ONE pure
expression in one place; and duplicated work is already absorbed by the
edge gather and the union-find, so the protocol does not have to be exact.

UTILITY: real on the GPU arm, mis-targeted on the CPU arm.  On GPU the walk
window is 581 ms of a 2863 ms iteration, per-PE walk time is 53.5 ms mean
against a 369 ms MAX (6.89x), and in the tail the busiest PEs are 63-81%
occupied BY THE WALK.  On CPU the tail is dominated by union-find, not the
walk, 3:1 -- reassigning walk pairs would not touch it.

CLARIFICATION: the load is 94% predictable from the data alone (r = 0.939
between two different binaries at two different leaf sizes), which means a
cheaper static reassignment may capture most of the win without any handoff
protocol.  Three questions at the end.

====================================================================
1.  YOUR PREMISE IS CORRECT, AND HERE IS ITS SIZE
====================================================================

Walk = entry method `goDown(travIdx)`.  NOT `startDual`, which is only the
launcher and carries ~0.1% of the time; the walk body runs in goDown, re-
entered after every remote node fetch.

    GPU arm (FoF3.gpu-prodtr, ppn 7, -l 128, fix on)
      walk window                581 ms   (iteration 2863 ms -> 20.3%)
      walk CPU                 47968 ms over 896 PEs = 53.5 ms/PE
      per-PE  p50 40.1  p90 119.0  p99 213.0  MAX 369.1 ms   max/mean 6.89x
      PEs doing NO walk at all: 11

    CPU arm (FoF3.cpu-prodtr, ppn 7, -l 32)
      walk window                575 ms   (iteration 4938 ms -> 11.6%)
      walk CPU                 21967 ms over 896 PEs = 24.5 ms/PE
      per-PE  p50 21.4  p90 49.0  p99 84.5  MAX 97.4 ms      max/mean 3.97x

THE WALK IS NEVER DENSE.  Peak participation is 45% of PEs (GPU) and 42%
(CPU).  It is not that the walk starts wide and narrows -- it never uses
more than half the machine at any instant.

    GPU, active PEs across the walk window, 10 slices
      0-10%  368   10-20%  402   20-30%  274   30-40%  129   40-50%   44
     50-60%   13   60-70%    5   70-80%    3   80-90%    2  90-100%    1

    71% of the window is spent with fewer than 25% of PEs walking, and that
    tail carries only 23.6% of the walk CPU.  Machine-wide utilisation in
    the last 349 ms is 1.82%.

====================================================================
2.  THE ONE MEASUREMENT THAT DECIDES IT: IS THE TAIL LOADED OR WAITING?
====================================================================

A long thin tail can be imbalance (a few PEs with too much work) or a
dependence chain (a few PEs waiting on round trips).  Reassignment fixes
the first and does nothing for the second.  They look identical in a
utilisation plot.  So: what are the tail PEs actually DOING?

    GPU tail, 349 ms, by entry method (PE-ms across all 896)
        3456.1  goDown                    <-- THE WALK, dominant
        1136.8  insertDataFindBoss
         326.9  requestNodes
         279.9  union_requests
         172.3  addCache
      busiest PEs: 840 at 81.3% busy, 343 at 76.7%, 825 68.7%, 364 68.6%,
                   342 63.6%, 854 49.7%, 791 46.8%, 350 43.3%

    CPU tail, 275 ms
         712.8  insertDataFindBoss        <-- UNION-FIND, dominant
         225.0  goDown
         118.4  requestNodes
         108.4  union_requests
      busiest PE 350 at 70.8% busy

*** ON THE GPU ARM THE TAIL IS THE WALK AND THE STRAGGLERS ARE GENUINELY
    LOADED -- 63-81% busy.  That is imbalance, and your scheme addresses
    it directly.

*** ON THE CPU ARM THE TAIL IS THE UNION-FIND FIND-BOSS CHAIN, 3.2x more
    than the walk.  Moving walk pairs around would leave it untouched.
    Note also that on CPU the busiest PE spends only 97 ms of a 575 ms
    window walking (17%), so even a perfect walk reassignment could not
    compress that window much. ***

====================================================================
3.  THE OVERLOAD IS DATA-DETERMINED, NOT TRANSIENT
====================================================================

Per-PE walk time, CPU arm against GPU arm -- different binary, different
leaf size (32 vs 128), different run, different nodes:

      Pearson r = 0.939 over 896 PEs
      top-25 busiest PEs: 17 in common   (chance ~0.7)
      top-50 busiest PEs: 31 in common   (chance ~2.8)
      top-100:            74 in common   (chance ~11.2)

The same PEs are heavy in both arms.  The overload is a property of WHICH
TREE PIECES LANDED WHERE and of the parity tie-break, not of machine noise.

Concentration (GPU): the top 100 PEs, 11% of the machine, hold 32.7% of
all walk CPU; the top 25 hold 11.4%.

That cuts both ways for your proposal, and it is the main thing I would
want your judgement on -- see section 6.

====================================================================
4.  FEASIBILITY
====================================================================

WHAT IS ALREADY IN YOUR FAVOUR:

(a) The unit of work is already exactly what you describe.  DualTraverser
    carries a stack of (source node, target payload) pairs and expands it
    with `nodes.emplace(node->getChild(i), curr_payload)` (Traverser.h).
    A handoff message is two node keys plus the traversal index.  Nothing
    has to be invented to name the work.

(b) The tie-break is ONE expression, in one place (Traverser.h ~806):

        int target_piece = tp.thisIndex, source_piece = node->tp_index;
        bool keep = (((target_piece + source_piece) & 1) == 0)
                        ? (target_piece < source_piece)
                        : (target_piece > source_piece);
        if (!keep) continue;

    Parity of the index sum picks the side.  It is deliberately balanced in
    COUNT -- each piece keeps about half its counterparts -- and completely
    blind to LOAD.  Making it load-aware is a local change.

(c) DUPLICATED WORK IS ALREADY SAFE.  The design comment on
    SkipMirroredPairs states that "the gather root and the union-find
    library both absorb any remaining multiplicity."  So if a handed-off
    pair races with local execution, or a pair is walked on both sides, the
    answer is still exact -- you lose time, not correctness.  This is a big
    deal: the protocol can be best-effort rather than transactional.

(d) The receiver needs no new data path.  It holds one side locally and
    fetches the other through the ordinary cache (requestNodes/addCache),
    which is what the current owner does for its remote half anyway.  The
    roles simply transpose.

WHAT HAS TO BE BUILT, AND WHERE THE RISK IS:

(e) COMPLETION DETECTION.  The walk finishes via armWalkCompletion /
    fireWalkDone.  Migrated pairs must be counted where they end up, or the
    walk either finishes early (wrong answer) or never (hang).  This is the
    real work in the proposal, not the entry method.

(f) THE RECEIVER MUST NOT RE-APPLY THE TIE-BREAK and bounce the pair back.
    The handoff needs a flag saying "you own this, the rule is overridden".

(g) INTERACTION WITH THE TWO EXISTING PRUNES.  SkipLocalSource discards a
    pair whose source is local, justified by phase-1 completeness; and
    peSetKeepLocalPair already vetoes that prune narrowly for the PE-set
    split.  A pair arriving from elsewhere transposes which side is local,
    so it must be exempted from SkipLocalSource or it will be silently
    dropped on arrival -- and a silently dropped pair is a WRONG ANSWER,
    not a slow one.  This is the subtle failure mode; it would show up as
    components high, exactly the signature recorded when the PE-set split
    first broke that prune (10k gate 3642 against gold 3549).

(h) You said "not at the level of whole treepiece pairs, because that might
    have been walked down already" -- agreed, and the trace supports it:
    the handoff has to name an interior pair, which means the sender must
    be able to serialise a stack entry mid-descent.  That is easy for the
    pair itself, but any per-walk memo state (the SEEN suppression the
    design notes rely on for the 8-way alternating split) does not travel
    with it.  Worst case that costs re-expansion on the receiver, not
    incorrectness -- but it should be measured, not assumed.

====================================================================
5.  UTILITY -- WHAT IT IS WORTH IF IT WORKS PERFECTLY
====================================================================

    GPU arm.  Walk CPU is 53.5 ms/PE.  If every pair were spread perfectly
    the walk's compute would be ~54 ms instead of a 581 ms window.  The
    walk sits inside a 1214.6 ms Tree traversal in a 2863 ms iteration.

      perfect (unreachable)   -527 ms   -18% of the iteration
      halving the window      -290 ms   -10%
      taking the top-100 PEs' excess    -150 to -200 ms   -5 to -7%

    I would plan against the middle row and treat -10% as the honest target.
    That is the same order as the affinity fix (-23%) and worth doing.

    CPU arm.  The equivalent ceiling is -550 ms of a 4938 ms iteration, but
    the tail is union-find, so the achievable number is much smaller and I
    would not start here.

    CAVEAT I CANNOT REMOVE FROM THE TRACES: the walk window is 581 ms and
    the enclosing Tree traversal is 1214.6 ms, so ~630 ms of that phase is
    something other than goDown.  Compressing the walk shortens the
    ITERATION only to the extent the walk is on the critical path.  The
    utilisation collapse to 1.8% during the tail says nothing else is
    running then, which is good evidence it IS the critical path, but a
    direct test (artificially shorten the walk, see if the iteration
    moves) would settle it for the cost of one job.

====================================================================
6.  CLARIFICATION -- THREE QUESTIONS BACK
====================================================================

Q1. WHICH ARM ARE YOU AIMING AT?  On GPU the tail is the walk and your
    scheme fits.  On CPU the tail is insertDataFindBoss and it does not.
    If the CPU arm is the target, the same style of question applied to
    the union-find find-boss chain is where the time is.

Q2. GIVEN r = 0.939, DO YOU WANT DYNAMIC HANDOFF OR A BETTER STATIC RULE?
    The load is predictable from the data.  That means a load-aware
    replacement for the parity tie-break -- weighting by piece particle
    count, or by measured walk cost from a previous iteration -- could
    capture much of the win with no new entry method, no completion-
    detection change, and none of the risk in (e) and (g) above.  The
    dynamic scheme is strictly more general and strictly more dangerous.
    My instinct is to try the static rule first BECAUSE it is measurable
    against the same traces before any code is written; but this is a
    judgement about how much generality you want, and it is yours.

Q3. WHAT TRIGGERS A HANDOFF?  A PE with a deep backlog does not know
    anyone is idle, and an idle PE does not know who is loaded.  Push on
    overload (I have more than K pending pairs, shed to the counterpart)
    needs no global state and cannot deadlock; pull on idle needs a work
    -stealing directory.  Push looks right here because the overload is
    predictable and concentrated -- 11% of PEs hold 33% of the work -- but
    I want to know whether you were thinking of a threshold, a periodic
    check, or something driven by the quiescence machinery.

13. [SUPERSEDED by items 20-26 -- the no-barrier row does NOT describe a
    stage-splitting scheme.  Kept for the arithmetic.]
    *** THE BARRIER IS THE PROBLEM. ***  Model: spread the self stage (and
    the ~10% of phaseA outside both stages) perfectly over the 14 PEs, then
    each PE does its own owner-bound cross work.  New phaseA wall:
                                     16 nodes            64 nodes
       today                         2.004 s             0.522 s
       WITH a barrier between stages 1.922 s (-82 ms,    0.536 s (+14 ms,
                                     -1.6% of Iter0)     +0.5% -- A LOSS)
       NO barrier (cross overlaps)   1.636 s (-368 ms,   0.424 s (-98 ms,
                                     -7.1% of Iter0)     -3.5% of Iter0)
       perfect everywhere            1.147 s (-857 ms)   0.265 s (-257 ms)

14. WHY.  Algebraically  after/floor = 1 + (cross_sum/pa_sum) x (cross_skew - 1).
    With cross at 5-8% of phaseA and skew ~2.7 that is +9% to +12% -- the
    same size as the 1.09-1.10 self skew being removed.  A barrier turns the
    cross stage's skew into an ADDITIVE cross_max term.  At 64 nodes it adds
    back slightly more than the rebalance saves.

15. WITHOUT the barrier the scheme reaches the within-process floor EXACTLY,
    at every process, at both scales.  cross_max never exceeds a process's
    own floor anywhere: median 0.117x the floor at 16 nodes (max 0.334x),
    median 0.169x at 64 nodes (max 0.724x).  0 of 128 and 0 of 512 processes
    have cross_max above their floor.  There is always room to hide the
    cross work behind the self tail.

16. [WITHDRAWN -- see item 25.]  RECOMMENDATION: if the scheme is built, self and cross must NOT be
    separated by a barrier -- cross work has to be allowed to run against
    the tail of the self stage.  That is the difference between -7.1% and
    -1.6% at 16 nodes, and between -3.5% and a regression at 64.

17. WHERE THE PLATEAU'S EXCESS SITS (relay10 item 50c, top 19 by pa_max_s).
    Excess of the plateau over the rest, split by stage:
       16 nodes: self +5.57 s (88.9% of excess, +43% relative)
                 cross +0.70 s (11.1% of excess, +102% relative)
                 proportional would be self 95.0% / cross 5.0%
       64 nodes: self +1.56 s (83.6%, +52% relative)
                 cross +0.31 s (16.4%, +118% relative)
                 proportional would be self 92.1% / cross 7.9%
    Two readings, both true.  In ABSOLUTE seconds the plateau's excess is

    process even though it is not the bulk of its excess.

20. YOUR SECOND READING IS THE RIGHT ONE, AND IT DOES WORK.  Cross pair
    (i,j) becomes available the moment the LATER of i,j finishes its self
    walk; each pair is then enumerated exactly once, when its second
    endpoint lands.  Suppression survives, because the memo that makes
    cross(i,j) cheap is built by self(i) and self(j) -- both done by
    construction.  So no barrier is structurally required.  That much of
    what I said stands.

21. BUT CROSS IS OWNER-BOUND FOR A DIFFERENT REASON THAN I IMPLIED, AND IT
    IS NOT A BARRIER.  phaseAAdmit (fof/FoFPhase1.h:2004) appends each
    claimed piece to a PE-LOCAL flat uf_parent array at its own offset;
    certRep(a,sa) and connectedRep(a,sa) index into that array.  A cross
    pair can only be walked by a PE holding BOTH pieces in its own
    uf_parent.  That is an ADDRESSING constraint, not a synchronisation
    one.  Removing a barrier does not remove it.

22. THE REAL ERROR: SELF AND CROSS ARE NOT INDEPENDENTLY SCHEDULABLE.
    Claiming a piece BOTH assigns it AND self-walks it.  A PE holding p
    pieces owes p(p-1)/2 cross pairs, so taking one more piece to fill
    idle time adds one self walk AND p-1 cross pairs.  The unit of
    movement is a piece and it carries both stages.  My no-barrier model,
    max(pa_sum/14, cross_max), assumed you could move self without moving
    cross.  You cannot.  It therefore does not describe any self/cross
    stage-splitting scheme, with or without a barrier.

23. WHAT THE -7.1% / -3.5% ACTUALLY IS.  max(pa_sum/14, cross_max) with
    cross_max never binding (0 of 128 and 0 of 512 processes have
    cross_max above their own floor) is just the PER-PROCESS FLOOR.  So it
    is the ceiling of PERFECT ASSIGNMENT of pieces to PEs within a
    process -- the ceiling of a better CLAIM POLICY.  Same number,
    different mechanism, and the mechanism is what tells you what to build.

24. AND THE SELF STAGE IS ALREADY BALANCED, BY MACHINERY ALREADY ON.  Both
    jobs ran FOF_STEALA=1 / FOF_STEALA_GEO=1, and the pool is working:
       16 nodes: 56,125 own claims, 7,821 foreign = 12.2% foreign,
                 mean 10.7 of 14 PEs per process stealing (range 6-13)
       64 nodes: 212,578 own, 31,268 foreign = 12.8% foreign,
                 mean 10.5 of 14 (range 5-13)
    The 1.09-1.10 self skew is the RESIDUAL AFTER STEALING.  There is no
    large untapped self imbalance for a self-stealing scheme to harvest.
    THAT, not the barrier, is why the stage split is worth so little.

25. WITHDRAWN: item 16's "build it barrier-free".  The barrier is not the
    binding constraint and removing it buys nothing on its own.  What
    survives from the measurement: the stage split WITH a barrier is worth
    -1.6% at 16 nodes and a small loss at 64 (item 13's first row is still
    right), the cross stage really is skewed 2.7x and really is
    owner-bound, and 57-58% of the excess really is across-process.

26. WHAT I WOULD BUILD INSTEAD, if anything within-process.  The claim pool
    takes own pieces first, then nearest-centroid among unclaimed
    (fof/FoFPhase1.h:2089).  It balances pieces TAKEN; it never prices the
    MARGINAL CROSS COST of taking one (p-1 new pairs against the p already
    held).  A claim priority that charges that cost is small, local and
    testable, and item 23 says the headroom is -368 ms at 16 nodes,
    -98 ms at 64.
    Unmeasured upside: total cross work is CONVEX in pieces-per-PE
    (sum p_i(p_i-1)/2), so equalising piece counts should REDUCE total
    cross work, not merely redistribute it.
    THE ONE THING MISSING to settle this: the instrument reports
    per-process sums and maxima only.  A per-PE (pieces, self, cross) dump
    would let me correlate them directly.  Say the word and I will add it
    -- it is a few lines in the same deposit -- but it needs a rebuild and
    a job, so I have not done it unasked.

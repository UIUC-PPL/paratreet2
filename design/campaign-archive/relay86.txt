====================================================================
3.  QUESTION 1: DOES CONTRACTION REPAIR SERIAL'S PENALTY?  MOSTLY, YES
====================================================================

    gathered edges   B  1,227,700 unique        C  487,352 contracted   -60%
    serial finish    B  1.197 s                 C  0.353 s              -70%
    penalty vs dist  B  +1488.8 ms  +33.2%      C  +297.0 ms  +6.6%

**Contraction removes 80% of serial's penalty** (1488.8 -> 297.0 ms).  The
mechanism is exactly the predicted one: retiring the same-process edges the
PE-set split manufactures, per process, before anything is shipped.  The
serial finisher is superlinear in its input -- 40% of the edges cost 30% of
the time -- so the volume cut pays slightly better than proportionally.

THE SPLIT, MEASURED (FOF3STAT gather, both reps):

    local 739,222 / 738,994    cross 495,102 / 495,105    contracted 487,352

Predicted was ~790k local / ~455k cross; measured is 739k / 495k, so local is
60% rather than the 63.5% relay83 reported.  The two are counting slightly
different things and neither is wrong: relay83 counted uf2 SUBMISSIONS
(union_request's same-chare fast path against distributed chains), while this
counts STAGED EDGES classified by `tip >> kUF2IdxBits` on both endpoints.
The ~6% gap between them is worth remembering before quoting either as "the"
local fraction.

====================================================================
4.  QUESTION 2: DOES C APPROACH OR BEAT A?  APPROACHES, DOES NOT BEAT
====================================================================

C is +6.6% and the ranges are separated, so this is not a tie.  The accounting
in section 2 says why, and both halves were predicted:

    forfeits streaming overlap        -132 ms of walk concurrency
    pays a serial finisher            +315 ms against dist's 0.038 s residue

So gather removes the drain's round-trips -- dist's uf2 residue plus the
cascade inside its walk -- and replaces them with a 0.353 s serial stage on
PE 0.  At 2B/16 nodes that trade is a 297 ms loss.  Note the direction of
scaling: the serial finisher grows with the number of processes while dist's
cascade does not, so this gap widens with scale, exactly as the design note
says ("anti-scales with process count by construction").

====================================================================
5.  QUESTION 3: WHAT THE ROOT-PAIR COLLAPSE BUYS -- ALMOST NOTHING
====================================================================

    cross 495,102  ->  contracted 487,352      ratio 0.9843

*** THE COLLAPSE REMOVES 1.6%.  That is the number Kale asked for to feed the
    hierarchical variant, and it is far smaller than the framing assumed. ***

What it means concretely: after each process replaces its locally-owned
endpoints with their local roots, essentially every cross edge still names a
DISTINCT pair of roots.  Local components are fine-grained relative to the
cross edges -- two cross edges from the same process rarely share both
endpoints' local roots.  There is no redundancy in the cross set to squeeze.

*** AND THIS IS THE PART THAT MATTERS FOR THE HIERARCHICAL VARIANT, STATED
    CAREFULLY.  C's win over B is NOT the collapse; it is the local/cross
    split -- 739k edges retired because both endpoints were same-process.
    Only 7,750 more came off through dedup.  A hierarchical variant would
    therefore gain from RETIRING EDGES INTERNAL TO A COARSER GROUP (say a
    node: 8 processes), not from further dedup, and this job does not measure
    that quantity.  What fraction of the 495k cross-process edges are also
    cross-NODE is unmeasured and is the number the hierarchical variant lives
    or dies on.  If most cross-process edges stay within a node, a node-level
    stage would retire most of them and the remaining gather would be small;
    if they are mostly cross-node, the hierarchy adds a stage and buys little.
    That is one cheap census away (classify staged cross edges by
    tip >> kUF2IdxBits mapped to node) and I have not run it. ***

====================================================================
6.  WHAT I WOULD SAY ABOUT -u gather
====================================================================

  - It is CORRECT at 2B, bitwise, and that is a real result for the algebra.
  - It repairs 80% of serial's penalty and confirms the same-process edge
    inflation as the cause, which was the diagnosis in relay78 section 11.
  - It does not beat dist and it is not close enough to be a candidate: +6.6%
    with separated ranges, and the gap grows with process count.
  - The collapse it was partly premised on buys 1.6%, so the hierarchical
    variant needs a different justification than "collapse more" -- it needs
    the cross-node fraction, which is unmeasured.

NOT MEASURED: any scale other than 16 nodes; the GPU arm; and the cross-node
fraction of cross-process edges (section 5).
